Social media strategy: what to do in the first 90 days

4 September 2026 · 3 min read

Social media is the cheapest way to make a brand visible — and the fastest way to waste your time if you don't have a plan. Here's a simple 90-day framework, starting from nothing.

First things first: likes aren't the goal

Follower counts and likes are vanity metrics. The real goal is attention, trust, and finally an enquiry that buys. Measure every decision against that — not against whether the number looks nice.

Organic or paid? They're not the same job

Before you start anything, it's worth keeping the two apart, because their timescales are completely different:

  • Organic community building — measured in months. It builds recognition and trust, which then supports every other channel. That's what this article is about.
  • Paid client acquisition — measured in campaign cycles, typically 30 days. If you need enquiries now, this is the faster route: this is how we win clients.

Neither replaces the other. Advertising brings the enquiry; your organic presence decides what someone finds when they look you up — which is exactly why it's fine that community building is slower.

Days 0–30: laying the ground

  • Pick 1–2 platforms, where your audience actually is. Better to stand out on one than to be half-present on five.
  • Sort out the profile: a clear name, picture and bio that says in one sentence who you help and with what, plus a contact detail or link.
  • Define the tone: how does your brand speak? It shows up in everything, from posts to replies.
  • Look at the competition: what works for them and what doesn't — don't copy it, learn from it.

Days 30–60: content and rhythm

  • Build 3–4 content pillars: for example education (tips), trust (case studies, behind the scenes), product/service, and personality. That way you won't run out of ideas.
  • Have a schedule: three good posts a week you can sustain beats one a day that runs out after a fortnight.
  • Short video leads the way: organic reach on Reels/TikTok/Shorts is the strongest right now. You don't need a professional studio — you need a clear message and a good first 2 seconds.
  • Manage the community: reply to comments and messages. Both the algorithm and people reward genuine interaction.

Days 60–90: measure and accelerate

  • Look at what worked: which topic, format and post brought saves, shares and messages. Do more of that.
  • Boost the winners with money — but only after tracking is in place: get conversion tracking working first, or you're just buying reach. (More on this: what Google Ads costs.) Then it's worth amplifying the best-performing organic post — not advertising at random.
  • Keep a simple report: reach, engagement and — what actually matters — how many enquiries or messages came in. That last one is the only number you should move budget on; the rest is just signal.

Common mistakes

  • The same content on every platform, unchanged.
  • Only selling, with no value given. As a rule of thumb an 80% value / 20% offer ratio tends to work — that's experience, not a measured result.
  • Giving up after two weeks because “it isn't working”. Community building is measured in months, not days.

The bottom line

Few platforms, a clear tone, a sustainable rhythm, and measurement at the end — that's the difference between “we post things” and “social media brings us clients”. If you'd rather not run it yourself: this is how we handle social media.
